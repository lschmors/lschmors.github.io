From August 2-8, 2026, I attended this year's [Deep Learning Indaba](https://deeplearningindaba.com/) in Lagos, Nigeria.
I also organized the satellite workshop on NeuroAI together with a team of [Imbizo](https://imbizo.africa/) students.

The Deep Learning Indaba is the largest machine learning and AI conference on the African continent. It brings together people from research and industry to build the future of AI in Africa.
Our satellite workshop was about NeuroAI, what research happens at the intersection of neuroscience and machine learning, but also who gets to do it, and what it takes to grow that expertise on the African continent.

The workshop opening was by Dr. AZA Allsop (Yale University) with a talk on "Building African NeuroAI Capacity", followed with keynote talks by Dr. Devon Jarvis from the Machine Intelligence and Neural Discovery (MIND) Institute, who spoke about "Understanding Language Evolution from Humans to LLMs", and Dr. Jonathan Shock from the University of Cape Town, who turned the usual question around and asked what we can learn about ourselves by studying AI.

Then, in a hands-on tutorial, participants worked through a Colab notebook on training and evaluating foundation models for neuroscience, so everyone left with something they could keep using.

We closed with a panel discussion on future directions in neuroscience and AI, career paths, and capacity building on the African continent.

''Thank you to all participants for the engaging questions and for the great atmosphere in the room. The excitement about machine learning in neuroscience was obvious. And the panel discussion was probably as empowering for those of us answering as for the people asking.''

This was funded by the collaborative research center “Robust Vision – Inference Principles and Neural Mechanisms” (CRC 1233).
